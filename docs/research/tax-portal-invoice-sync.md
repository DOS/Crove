# Purchase-invoice sync from the tax portal

> Spike decision note for [#322](https://github.com/DOS/Ludy/issues/322) (backlog review item M2-14), which gates the M3 daily sync (#325, item M3-3 in [backlog-review-2026-10-17.md](../plan/backlog-review-2026-10-17.md)). Desk research on 2026-10-19: vendor help pages, legal-summary sites, government news and public GitHub projects. Nobody logged in to a real portal, no scraper was written, and no CAPTCHA was touched. Vendor pages are vendor claims. **Inference** marks a conclusion drawn from the sources rather than stated by them; **unverified** marks a point that no source confirmed. The legal reading is not legal advice; sections 3 and 7 list what a lawyer must confirm. Vietnamese summary for the owner: [tax-portal-invoice-sync.vi.html](tax-portal-invoice-sync.vi.html).

## 1. Summary

- **There is no official way for a buyer, or software acting for a buyer, to pull its purchase invoices from the tax authority.** The e-invoice portal ([hoadondientu.gdt.gov.vn](https://hoadondientu.gdt.gov.vn)) offers a logged-in lookup with Excel export and one-at-a-time XML download, behind a password and a CAPTCHA on every login. Registered access outside the portal is for state bodies, credit institutions, e-invoice providers and organisations a taxpayer authorises, each under its own agreement with the tax authority (sections 2.3 and 3); none of them is a buyer API.
- **Every vendor that "syncs from the tax authority" uses the client's own portal password** and drives the portal's undocumented internal endpoints, the same way public open-source tools do. MISA runs the sync from a desktop helper on the customer's PC; Sapo syncs every 4 hours from its servers; standalone tools advertise automatic CAPTCHA reading. In 2026 the portal added rate limiting (HTTP 429), a WAF, a redesign and new request requirements, and tool authors report repeated breakage (section 2).
- **The legal position is a grey zone, not a clear yes.** Decree 254/2026 (which replaced Decree 123/2020 on 2026-07-01) lets buyers use the portal and sets up a separate, registered route for "authorised organisations" acting for a taxpayer, with their own accounts. Using the client's own password sits outside that route: nothing found forbids it in terms, the decree tells the taxpayer to protect its login, and no portal terms of use were found. Solving the CAPTCHA by software is the part most likely to be read as circumventing a protection measure (section 3).
- **Recommendation: no-go for M3-3 as written** (an unattended daily sync with a sealed password). Every login needs a CAPTCHA, so an unattended sync means solving CAPTCHAs by software, which this project will not do. **Go for a reshaped M3-3:** an *assisted* sync where the accountant, once a week or month per client, solves the CAPTCHA in Crove and Crove fetches that period's purchase invoices with a short-lived session, never storing the password. Before that, a small M2 item: import the portal's own Excel list so Crove can show which invoices of the month are still missing (section 6).
- **Layer:** the assisted sync can live in the open-source core as an opt-in provider that is off by default; it needs no DOS service. The owner decides whether Crove publishes a client for a government portal in a public repo or keeps it in Crove Cloud (section 7, decision O2).

## 2. How vendors do it today

### 2.1 The portal

- Login is the company tax code as user name, the password the tax authority emailed at registration, and a CAPTCHA; then Tra cứu, Tra cứu hóa đơn, and the purchase ("mua vào") or sales ("bán ra") list ([MISA help](https://helpv4.meinvoice.vn/kb/tra-cuu-hoa-don-dien-tu-tren-trang-tra-cuu-cua-tong-cuc-thue/), [vendor guide](https://www.meinvoice.vn/tin-tuc/771/tra-cuu-hoa-don-dien-tu/)). Repeated wrong passwords lock the account; recovery is through "Quên mật khẩu" ([MISA help](https://helpact.misa.vn/kb/xu-ly-truong-hop-tai-khoan-dang-nhap-trang-hoadondientu-gdt-gov-bi-khoa-hoac-quen-mat-khau-dang-nhap/)).
- The result list exports to Excel; XML and PDF download one invoice at a time, with no native bulk download. Vendors advise splitting lookups into short ranges near filing deadlines because the portal slows down ([reseller guide](https://minhvnpt.com/cach-tai-hoa-don-dien-tu-hang-loat-tu-cong-thue)).
- Tool builders report, from the internal API: pages of about 50 results with a cursor, a search range of about one month, a CAPTCHA valid for about 2 minutes, and a bearer token after login ([Giải Pháp Excel forum thread](https://www.giaiphapexcel.com/diendan/threads/t%E1%BA%A3i-h%C3%B3a-%C4%91%C6%A1n-%C4%91i%E1%BB%87n-t%E1%BB%AD-https-hoadondientu-gdt-gov-vn-excel-vba.171723/)). How long that token lasts is **unverified**; one project calls it short-lived ([vatengine](https://github.com/luutuanvu1010/vatengine)).
- 2026 changes seen by those builders: a redesign (May), a new request identifier (September), HTTP 429 rate limiting and HTML block pages served with status 200, and some servers outside Vietnam unable to reach the site (same forum thread; [TaiHoaDonDienTu](https://github.com/dieutx/TaiHoaDonDienTu), [KE-TOAN](https://github.com/dothaihung92/KE-TOAN)). A community API test suite treats WAF blocks as a known failure ([hoadondientu-auto-test](https://github.com/NLHGiang/hoadondientu-auto-test)).
- Identity is tightening around VNeID: since a May 2026 tax authority letter, the legal representative passes a biometric check when registering or changing e-invoice use ([lsvn.vn](https://lsvn.vn/nguoi-dai-dien-theo-phap-luat-phai-xac-thuc-sinh-trac-hoc-khi-dang-ky-hoa-don-dien-tu-a173250.html)), and the national public service portal accepts only e-identity accounts from 2026-07-20 ([luatvietnam.vn](https://luatvietnam.vn/tin-van-ban-moi/tu-20-7-2026-chi-su-dung-tai-khoan-dinh-danh-dien-tu-de-dang-nhap-cong-dich-vu-cong-quoc-gia-186-110666-article.html)). Whether the invoice lookup login itself will move to VNeID is **unverified**; if it does, a stored password stops working (**inference**).
- The General Department of Taxation became the Tax Department (Cục Thuế) under the Ministry of Finance on 2025-03-01; the domain is still `gdt.gov.vn` ([einvoice.vn](https://einvoice.vn/tin-tuc/tong-cuc-thue-chinh-thuc-doi-ten-thanh-cuc-thue-tu-ngay-01-3-2025)). eTax Mobile has no purchase-invoice lookup for companies that could be found ([soha.vn](https://soha.vn/tin-vui-cho-hang-trieu-ca-nhan-ho-kinh-doanh-dang-su-dung-etax-mobile-198261008083504321.htm)).

Public open-source tools show the technique the vendors do not describe: they call the portal's internal JSON endpoints for the CAPTCHA, the login and the queries. Some read the CAPTCHA by matching its SVG glyphs or with OCR ([hddt-downloader-windows](https://github.com/dieutx/hddt-downloader-windows), [TaiHoaDonDienTu](https://github.com/dieutx/TaiHoaDonDienTu)); others have the user type it and say they do not bypass it ([hddt_conn](https://github.com/tudn1986/hddt_conn), [KE-TOAN](https://github.com/dothaihung92/KE-TOAN), [vatengine](https://github.com/luutuanvu1010/vatengine)). vatengine keeps only the portal's token, envelope-encrypted, and asks the user to log in again when it expires, which is the closest public precedent for the design in section 4.

**Inference:** MISA's choice of a helper on the customer's PC means its requests come from the customer's own IP and session, which avoids server IP blocks and the WAF, and keeps MISA's servers out of the login. That is a deliberate trade, not an accident, and it is also the incumbent this feature is meant to match.

### 2.3 Official channels and provider APIs

- **Decree 123/2020 Articles 46 and 47** (in force until 2026-06-30): sellers and buyers use the portal with their own login; state bodies, credit institutions and e-invoice service providers that sign an information-exchange agreement get a direct, signed and encrypted connection ([summary](https://luathoanganh.vn/hoi-dap-thue/quy-dinh-ve-doi-tuong-cung-cap-thong-tin-su-dung-thong-tin-hoa-don-dien-tu-va-hinh-thuc-khai-thac-su-dung-lha1251.html)). Nothing found lets a provider pull a buyer's invoices on the buyer's behalf, and no provider advertises that as a product (**unverified**).
- **Decree 254/2026/ND-CP and Circular 91/2026/TT-BTC** replaced Decree 123/2020, Decree 70/2025 and Circular 32/2025 from 2026-07-01 ([EY bulletin](https://www.ey.com/vi_vn/technical/tax/tax-and-law-updates/nghi-dinh-254-2026-nd-cp-ve-hoa-don-dien-tu-va-chung-tu-dien-tu), [full text](https://xaydungchinhsach.chinhphu.vn/toan-van-nghi-dinh-so-254-2026-nd-cp-ve-hoa-don-dien-tu-chung-tu-dien-tu-119260713164251972.htm)). Its Articles 36 and 37 keep the Decree 123 model (portal for sellers and buyers, agreements or a direct query service for the others) and add "organisations authorised by" a taxpayer as a user class with its own registered access; section 3 reads them. No buyer API was found. The README still cites Decree 123/2020 and Circular 78/2021 as the XML basis; that is a separate follow-up (section 8).
- **Seller to buyer delivery is the legal baseline.** Since Decree 70/2025 the seller sends the invoice to the buyer electronically (email, message) or gives a link or QR code to look it up and download it ([meinvoice.vn](https://www.meinvoice.vn/?p=33843)). Crove already has a per-org inbound address that takes up to 20 attachments per email (`apps/kernel/src/inbound.ts`); emails that carry only a lookup link and code are not ingested today.
- **Provider APIs** (MISA meInvoice, Viettel S-Invoice, VNPT) are for issuing and for the seller's own invoices; a buyer receives invoices from dozens of providers, so they are no substitute for a buyer-side feed. A reseller page advertises a Viettel-ecosystem "purchase invoices by tax code" API through a third-party domain; it could not be reached (**unverified**).
- **T-VAN** providers carry tax filings; no source shows a T-VAN delivering purchase invoices to buyers ([lawnet](https://lawnet.vn/chinh-sach-thue/en/what-is-the-t-van-service-provider-in-the-field-of-taxation-in-vietnam-6279.html)). Becoming a provider or a T-VAN is out of scope (backlog review section 2).

## 3. Legal and terms-of-use position

Sources: secondary full-text reproductions and law-firm summaries; the signed texts in the official gazette were not read (thuvienphapluat.vn refused automated requests). Article numbers below are from those reproductions and must be checked against the gazette.

**Which rules apply now.** Decree 254/2026/ND-CP (45 articles, in force from 2026-07-01) implements the Law on Tax Administration 108/2025/QH15 and, by its Article 43, repeals Decree 123/2020, Article 1 of Decree 41/2022 and Decree 70/2025 ([government record](https://vanban.chinhphu.vn/?pageid=27160&docid=218689), [full-text reproduction](https://www.ketoanthienung.net/nghi-dinh-254-2026-nd-cp-huong-dan-ve-hoa-don-dien-tu.htm)). Circular 91/2026/TT-BTC replaces Circular 32/2025, which had replaced Circular 78/2021 ([einvoice.vn](https://einvoice.vn/tin-tuc/thong-tu-91-huong-dan-nghi-dinh-254)). Circular 89/2026/TT-BTC repeals Circular 19/2021 on electronic tax transactions ([luatvietnam.vn](https://luatvietnam.vn/tin-van-ban-moi/tu-01-7-2026-10-thong-tu-ve-thue-het-hieu-luc-thi-hanh-186-110487-article.html)). The personal data rules are now the Personal Data Protection Law 91/2025/QH15 and Decree 356/2025/ND-CP, both from 2026-01-01; Decree 356 replaced Decree 13/2023 ([EY](https://www.ey.com/vi_vn/technical/tax/tax-and-law-updates/nghi-dinh-so-356-2025-nd-cp-quy-dinh-chi-tiet-mot-so-dieu-va-bien-phap-thi-hanh-luat-bao-ve-du-lieu-ca-nhan), [law text](https://hethongphapluat.com/luat-bao-ve-du-lieu-ca-nhan-2025)). So the texts named in the issue (Decree 123/2020, Circular 78/2021, Decree 13/2023) are history; their successors say much the same on the points below.

**What the e-invoice decree says** (Decree 254, from the reproduction):

- Buyers may look up and obtain the original invoice file (Article 18.1); sellers must keep the portal user name and password the tax authority issued safe (Articles 15.1 and 16.1).
- Article 36.2 lists who may use invoice information: sellers and buyers; state agencies; credit institutions; e-invoice service providers; recipients of income tax withholding vouchers; foreign tax authorities; and (g) organisations **authorised by** an enterprise, household business or individual trader, as civil law allows.
- Article 37: sellers and buyers use the information through the tax system (the portal). State agencies, credit institutions, providers and authorised organisations get access under a written agreement with the tax authority or through a direct query service, each with a focal point registered with the tax authority, at most two accounts per organisation; providers on a direct query service must meet the criteria for invoice data transmission providers. The Minister of Finance sets the registration procedure.
- Article 39 binds every information user: use it only for its purpose, do not give information obtained from the tax authority to a third party without the consent of the tax authority or the account provider, register for access, and protect the login.

**Reading** (**inference**, for a lawyer to confirm):

1. **There is a formal route for a third party: register as an authorised organisation** (Articles 36.2(g) and 37.2). It is built for exactly the case of a firm acting for a taxpayer, but two things are open: whether point (g) covers access to a buyer's purchase invoices (its wording names the authorising taxpayer by its selling activity), and what Circular 91 requires to register (forms, criteria, whether an accounting firm qualifies). Neither was confirmed. If it works, the accounting firm (not Crove) would hold its own registered access, which removes the password-sharing problem; the partner's accountant is the right person to ask the local tax office.
2. **Using the client's own password is not forbidden in terms, but it sits outside that route.** The decree tells the taxpayer to protect its credentials (Articles 15, 16, 39.4), and Article 39.1 could be read as forbidding the client from passing portal data to a third party without consent. The Electronic Transactions Law 20/2023 attributes actions of a representative or an automated system the holder set up to the holder (Article 14.2) and does not invalidate automated transactions (Article 34), which supports treating consented access as the client's own act. The Law on Tax Administration 108/2025 lets taxpayers authorise others (Article 37.1) but keeps them responsible for errors by the authorised party (Article 37.2) ([law text](https://hethongphapluat.com/luat-quan-ly-thue-2025)). Net: the client carries the risk of what happens on its account, and Crove would act as its agent.
3. **No terms of use were found** for the portal: it is a single-page app with no published rules on automated access, bots or shared accounts. The only tax authority warnings found are about phishing, telling taxpayers not to give their password or OTP to fake emails or apps ([thitruongtaichinhtiente.vn](https://thitruongtaichinhtiente.vn/canh-bao-email-gia-mao-dia-chi-thu-dien-tu-nganh-thue-85727.html)). No enforcement against invoice download tools was found.
4. **CAPTCHA is the bright line.** Criminal Code Article 289 covers intrusion into another's network by getting past access codes, warnings or firewalls ([lsvn.vn](https://lsvn.vn/nghi-quyet-giai-thich-khoan-1-dieu-289-cua-bo-luat-hinh-su1638282571-a112400.html)); the Law on Cybersecurity 116/2025/QH15 (from 2026-07-01) lists unauthorised intrusion and disabling protection measures among prohibited acts (Article 7) ([law text](https://hethongphapluat.com/luat-an-ninh-mang-2025)); Decree 330/2026/ND-CP fines accessing data without authority or exploiting system weaknesses ([summary](https://pbgdpl.cantho.gov.vn/vi-pham-quy-dinh-ve-phong-chong-tan-cong-mang)). Access with the account holder's written consent is unlikely to be "unauthorised" towards the holder, but the system belongs to the tax authority: software that defeats its CAPTCHA, ignores a block page or retries past a rate limit is the part a prosecutor or the tax authority could call circumvention. No case law was found. Crove's design therefore keeps a human on every CAPTCHA and backs off on every block.
5. **Personal data.** Invoices of household businesses and individual buyers carry names, addresses, phone numbers and ID numbers, which are personal data (Law 91/2025 Article 2; Decree 356 Article 3). The client company is the controller of its invoices; the accounting firm processes for it; Crove is a processor for the firm, which needs a processing contract before it receives data (Article 37.2) and an impact assessment within 60 days of first processing (Articles 21 and 22). The small-business relief does not apply to a company that provides processing services (Article 38). Storing client data outside Vietnam is a cross-border transfer with its own assessment (Article 20), which supports hosting the pilot in Vietnam (backlog decision D3). These apply to every intake path, not only portal sync.
6. **Who would be at risk.** The client (its account, its duty to protect it), the firm (as the client's agent and the data processor), and Crove or DOS (as the operator of the software that logs in). An unattended stored-password design puts the most on Crove; the assisted design keeps a named member of the firm acting, with the client's recorded consent, at every login.

## 4. How a client's credential would be held

The channel layer (ADR-0025) already does most of what is needed; the differences come from what is being held.

**What is held.** A portal password is not an OAuth token: it has no scope, cannot be refreshed or revoked by Crove, and opens everything the client's portal account can do, which may include registering or changing the client's e-invoice use (**inference**: the same portal carries registration, and the 2026 biometric rule now guards that step). The design therefore prefers not to hold the password at all.

| Design | What Crove stores | CAPTCHA | Fits |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Session only (recommended) | Nothing durable: the password goes from the browser to the portal login through the kernel and is dropped; the portal's token is sealed for the length of one sync job and dropped when the job ends | Solved by the accountant in Crove, per sync | ADR-0025 seal and open, with a time limit |
| B. Sealed password, human CAPTCHA | The password, sealed like a channel token | Solved by the accountant per sync | Saves typing; the password then sits in Crove for months |
| C. Sealed password, software CAPTCHA | The password | Solved by software | Rejected: CAPTCHA bypass (section 3) |
| D. The firm's own login | Either a lookup-only portal user the client creates for the firm, if the portal supports it (**unverified**; the separate tax filing portal delegates staff through VNeID organisation identity, [thuvienphapluat](https://thuvienphapluat.vn/ma-so-thue/phap-luat-thue/phan-quyen-cho-ke-toan-dang-nhap-thuedientu-gdt-gov-vn-nhu-the-nao-609919-206576.html)), or the firm's own registered access as an authorised organisation (section 3, point 1); in both cases what Crove would hold is the firm's login, not the client's | Per sync | Best for least privilege if either exists; check with the partner (O5, O6) |

**Reusing ADR-0025 for design A or B:**

1. **Connection row.** A `channel_connection` with provider `gdt_einvoice` (the column is free text matching `^[a-z][a-z0-9_]{1,31}$`) and `external_account_id` = the client's tax code. One per org, since in Crove an org is one client company.
2. **Seal and open.** `CredentialVault.seal(secret, binding)` with binding (org, `gdt_einvoice`, tax code), so an envelope copied to another org does not open. `credential_put` and `credential_drop` are owners and admins of the org; `credential_get` is the worker only, in a system context, and refuses once the connecting member has left or lost the owner or admin role (`CHANNEL_OWNER_LEFT`). For a portal credential that rule is a feature: an accountant who leaves the firm takes the access with them, and the next one reconnects.
3. **Consent.** Connecting is an R2 command (`kernel.channel.connect`, permission `kernel.connection.bind`) by an owner or admin of the client's org. Because the password belongs to the client company, not to the accountant, the connect screen records a consent statement: who consents for the client (name, role), what Crove does (log in to the tax portal for this tax code and download purchase invoices for the chosen period), how often, and how to withdraw. The consent text version and time go in the command's audit row. The pilot agreement (M0-5) and the client's service contract with the firm are the legal basis; the screen only records it (**inference**: under the data protection rules in section 3, the firm is the controller and Crove the processor).
4. **Revocation.** `kernel.channel.disconnect` revokes the row and drops the envelope. The client can also revoke on its side at any time by changing the portal password; the next sync fails with an authentication error, the connection becomes `expired` with a reason, and the accountant sees it in the multi-client view (M2-6). Removing a member, disabling an account, offboarding the org (`docs/runbooks/org-offboarding.md`) and a credential key rotation (`docs/runbooks/credential-key.md`) already cover the envelope.
5. **Audit without secrets.** Audit rows carry the connection id, the tax code, the period, counts (found, new, duplicate, failed) and the portal's error class, never the password, the CAPTCHA answer, the token, cookies or raw response bodies. The redaction test of ADR-0025 extends to the new provider, and the provider's fetch is bound to the portal host only (`allowlistedFetch`).
6. **Rate and failure handling.** One sync per org at a time, a per-tax-code limit well under what tool builders report triggers HTTP 429, backoff on 429 or a block page, and no automatic retry of a failed login (a wrong password retried locks the client's account). Each outbound effect goes through `external_call` (ADR-0025 decision 5).
7. **Untrusted output.** Downloaded XML goes through the same `crove-extract` sandbox and duplicate checks as an upload; the portal's list is data, not instructions.

## 5. Core or Cloud

ADR-0027: the core runs with `docker compose up` and no DOS service, and the private `crove-cloud` layer holds DOS configuration and managed connectors.

- **What fits the core without question:** email ingestion, the bulk ZIP intake (M2-2), and importing the portal's Excel list (option 1 in section 6). They need no external service.
- **The assisted sync (option 2)** needs no DOS service either: the self-hosted kernel talks to the portal directly, from the firm's own server. By ADR-0027 it can be core, as an opt-in provider off by default (`TAX_PORTAL_SYNC=false`), shipped with the same allowlist and redaction rules as the social providers.
- **Reasons to keep it in Cloud instead:** a public AGPL client for an undocumented government endpoint invites forks that add a CAPTCHA solver; the endpoints change several times a year and a public repo makes every break visible; and Crove Cloud can run it from a Vietnamese IP and absorb the maintenance. The owner decides (section 7, O2).
- **A commercial connector** (buying sync from a vendor) would be Cloud by definition: it needs a contract and credentials DOS holds.

## 6. Go or no-go, estimate and alternatives

**Verdict.** No-go for an unattended daily sync with a stored password: it cannot work without software solving the CAPTCHA, the legal basis for third-party automated access is unconfirmed, and the portal is actively adding rate limits and blocks. Go, behind owner decisions O1 to O3 (and O5, which may replace the client password with the firm's own registered login), for a human-in-the-loop path that gets most of the time saving: the accountant does one CAPTCHA per client per period instead of downloading every invoice by hand.

**Alternatives ranked** (best first, for the MVP goal of 80% of a month's invoices entering Crove):

| Rank | Option | Layer | Effort | Value | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Portal list import plus email.** The accountant exports the month's purchase list from the portal (Excel), uploads it; Crove shows which invoices on the tax authority's list have no XML in Crove yet, per client (matching on seller tax code, symbol and number; that the export carries those columns and the status is an **inference** to check on a real export from the partner). **Suggested sequence.** Option 1 in M2 (it also feeds the VAT working, M2-4, with the tax authority's own totals). Option 2 as the reshaped M3-3 once O1 and O2 are answered; option 3 only if option 2 hits IP blocks or the owner wants Crove never to see a password.

**What would change the verdict.** A published API or data-sharing scheme for buyers under Decree 254 or Circular 91; a written legal opinion that software CAPTCHA solving with the taxpayer's consent is lawful (still against this project's rule, so it would need an owner decision); or the portal login moving to VNeID, which would end password-based designs and favour option 3.

## 7. Owner decisions

Each question carries the recommended answer. Per the working rule, a recommendation is adopted unless the owner objects.

- **O1. Does Crove accept the legal grey zone of logging in to the tax portal for a client, with that client's written consent and a human solving every CAPTCHA?** Context: no law found authorises or forbids third-party automated lookup with the taxpayer's own login; every competitor does it, most of them by solving the CAPTCHA by software, which Crove will not do; the tax filing rules treat the taxpayer as responsible for its account. Recommendation: yes for option 2, only after a Vietnamese lawyer confirms in writing the points in section 3 (a one-hour opinion, which can be added to the terms and privacy review already planned in M3-6), and only with the consent record of section 4.
- **O2. Core or Cloud for the assisted sync?** Context: ADR-0027 allows it in the core (no DOS service needed), but a public client for an undocumented government endpoint can be forked to add a CAPTCHA solver and breaks in public when the portal changes. Recommendation: core, opt-in and off by default, so self-hosting firms get the same feature, with the CAPTCHA always shown to a human and no solver hook in the code. Choose Cloud if the lawyer's opinion in O1 advises against publishing it.
- **O3. Store the password (design B) or never (design A)?** Context: storing it saves the accountant one field per sync but leaves a full-access client password in Crove for months, and every sync needs a human for the CAPTCHA anyway. Recommendation: never store it (design A).
- **O4. Reshape M3-3 from "daily sync" to "assisted sync per period", and add option 1 to M2?** Context: a daily sync without a human is not possible without CAPTCHA bypass; option 1 is small and removes the costly failure (missed invoices). Recommendation: yes to both; the coordinator retitles #325 and creates the option 1 issue.
- **O5. Ask the partner's accountant to check the registered route with their tax office?** Context: Decree 254 Article 36.2(g) and 37.2 let an organisation authorised by a taxpayer register for its own access (at most two accounts, a focal point, a written agreement or a direct query service), but whether that covers a firm reading its clients' purchase invoices, and what Circular 91 asks for, are unconfirmed. If it works, the firm logs in as itself and no client password is shared. Recommendation: yes, in parallel with O1; it costs one call or visit, and if the answer is yes the firm's own registered login becomes the default credential instead of the client's password.
- **O6. Ask the design partner two things in the M0 interviews (#57):** whether their clients would let the firm hold or type the portal password (and whether the portal lets the client create a lookup-only user for the firm), and how many minutes a month per client the portal download takes today. Recommendation: yes; the answers size the saving and settle design D.
